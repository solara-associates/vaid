VAID verifier

Check whether an agent credential is genuine, what it allows, and whether a chain of delegations holds together. Everything below runs in your browser. This page makes no network request while you verify. The only exception is "Fetch key from URL", which only runs when you click it.

Fetching a key URL is the one action on this page that reaches the network, and only when you click the button above. Everything else, including pasting, checking examples, and verifying, never leaves your browser.

What a "Pass" here proves, and what it doesn't

A pass means:

  • Every credential you pasted was genuinely signed by the key you provided, and has not been altered since.
  • None of them has passed its expiry date.
  • If you pasted a chain, each credential only narrows what the one before it was allowed to do. Nobody granted themselves more authority than they were given.

A pass does not mean:

  • That the credential hasn't been withdrawn. Revocation needs a live, trusted list to check against. This page is offline by design and has no such list, so it cannot tell you a credential was withdrawn five minutes ago. A pass here means "genuinely issued and in date," not "currently authorized."
  • That you can trust the public key. This page checks the signature against whatever key you gave it. If that key isn't really the issuer's, perhaps you got it from somewhere untrustworthy, a pass just means the credential matches that key, not that the key belongs to who you think.
  • Anything about what the agent actually did. This only checks the credential, not any request, log, or outcome.
